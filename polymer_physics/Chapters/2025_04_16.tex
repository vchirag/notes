\section*{Basic Polymer models}

Polymers are long chain-like, flexible structures of macromolecules. On large scales, their orientational correleations decay.\\

They can be ``physically" described by tangent vectors along a path. In some coordinate frame where the position of a point along a path $s$ is described by $\mathbf{r}$, the corresponding tangent vector $\mathbf{u}$ is:
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{u} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}(s)}{ds}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[h!]
	\includegraphics[width=0.4\linewidth]{imgs/2025_04_16/tangent_vector.png}
	\caption{Schematic of a polymer chain in some reference frame. The path length varies between $0$ and $1$.}
	\label{fig:tangent_vector}
\end{figure}

Since the orientational correleations are uncorreleated
\begin{equation}
	\langle\mathbf{u}(s_1)\mathbf{u}(s_2)\rangle = \mathcal{C}(s_1, s_2)
\end{equation}
where $\langle \dots \rangle$ denotes the ensemble average over all the points and $\mathcal{C}$ is a constant function.\\

\subsection*{Discrete Chain Model}
Assumes a polymer to be made up of balls connected by some threads/springs (see Figure \ref{fig:discrete_chain_model} ). Possible to describe the entire chain configuration by the position of the first bead $\mathbf{r}_1$ and the corresponding bond vectors for the subsequent beads $\mathbf{b}_i, \forall \,i \in \{1, \dots, (N-1)\}$.
